Who's counted in the 37,062 competing pool

  • Start: all 38,021 Telangana 2026 qualifiers.
  • Remove: All-India seat winners (real, published list).
  • Remove: candidates ruled ineligible for local quota.
  • Remove: AP APNL-area candidates.
  • Not removed: Cat A (CQ) applicants — applying isn't the same as getting a seat, so they're still real Cat B competitors.
  • → 37,062 real competitors remain.

Why this page opens on the conservative view

  • "Seats increase" defaults to Without.
  • The "With" scenario relies on a chain of assumptions about which candidates go where.
  • That chain helps top colleges (Apollo, Kamineni LB Nagar) the most, since they're earliest in the chain.
  • Switch to "With" only if you deliberately want the optimistic case.
  • For top colleges especially, treat "Without" as your real planning number.
How the refined pool and both scenarios are calculated

The basic idea

  • Colleges fill Management Quota seats in 3 rounds: Phase 1, Phase 2, Phase 3.
  • Phase 1 = toughest cutoff (strongest applicants only).
  • Phase 2 = a bit easier, only if seats remain.
  • Phase 3 = easiest, the last chance if seats still remain.
  • We don't have 2026 results yet — only 2025's — so everything below is how we estimate 2026's three marks.

Step 1 — who's still competing (the "37,062 pool")

  • Start: all 38,021 Telangana 2026 qualifiers.
  • Remove only confirmed non-competitors: All-India seat winners, and candidates ruled ineligible for local quota.
  • Keep Cat A (CQ) applicants in the pool — applying isn't the same as getting a seat.
  • Example: 100 qualify, 4 confirmed elsewhere, 2 ineligible → 94 real competitors, even if 40 of those 94 also applied for Cat A.
  • → Result: 37,062 real competitors.

Step 2 — turning a 2025 result into a 2026 estimate

  • Apollo's 2025 Phase 3 seat went to the student ranked 5,533rd of 41,584 (~top 13.3%).
  • We don't reuse rank 5,533 directly — this year's real pool (37,062) is a different size.
  • Instead: find who sits at that same top-13.3% mark within the 37,062 pool, and read their real 2026 score.
  • → For Apollo, this gives Phase 1/2/3 = 445 / 428 / 427.

"Without seat increase" (conservative, default view)

  • Just Step 2, with no further adjustment.
  • Answers: "if 2026 repeats 2025's pattern on this year's real pool, what marks are needed?"
  • Treat this as the safer number to plan around.

"With seat increase" (optimistic view) — two layers on top of Step 2

  • Layer A — this college's own seat growth. Apollo's seats grew 44→45 (just 1 seat) — barely moves anything, about 48 rank-positions of easing. This is a verified fact from the seat matrix, so it always applies at its real value and is not affected by the cascade dropdown below.
  • Layer B — the government college "domino effect." 4 government colleges added ~41 new seats this year.
  • Some students who'd have taken a paid Management Quota seat instead get one of these new government seats — one fewer real competitor for private colleges.
  • Traced through, this reaches Apollo specifically, adding up to 1,747 rank-positions of easing at full (100%) intensity, before the loyalty dampening below reduces it further — far bigger than Apollo's own seat growth.
  • → Apollo "With" at default settings (100% slide, +4% MQ) = 438 / 423 / 422, still below "Without" (445/428/427).
  • Honest caveat: this domino chain is the most speculative part of the model — it assumes displaced students pick "the next best option" in a predictable order, which we can't independently verify. Colored badges on each college show how confident we are; "high confidence" means "most defensible link in the chain," not "certain."

Cat B→Cat A cascade sliding dropdown

  • Controls how much of Layer B only (the government domino effect) to apply — 25%, 50%, or the full 100% currently modeled.
  • Layer A is never affected by this dropdown — a college's own real seat growth always applies at its true, measured value, since it's a fact, not an assumption.
  • Lower settings are for anyone who wants to trust the domino chain less — e.g., 25% assumes only a quarter of the displaced-candidate chain actually plays out as modeled.
  • 100% (the current default when "With seat increase" is on) matches everything described above with no discount applied.
  • This dropdown has no effect at all when "Seats increase" is set to "Without," since Layer B only exists in the "With" scenario.

Loyalty/"sticky demand" dampening (top Hyderabad colleges)

  • Checked real 2025 data: of Apollo's actual Cat B joiners, how many could have gotten some Cat A seat statewide but paid for Apollo's Cat B anyway?
  • → 59% of Apollo's joiners had a Cat A alternative and skipped it — real brand/location loyalty, not settling.
  • Checked the same for every other college: Kamineni LB Nagar 9%, Mamata Bachupally 11%, all other 20 colleges: 0% (their joiners had no real alternative at all).
  • Students who are loyal to a specific college wouldn't actually leave even if more government seats opened up — so the domino effect above only gets applied to the non-loyal share of each college's demand.
  • Apollo's domino relief is scaled to 41% of what it would otherwise be (100% − 59%); Kamineni LB Nagar to 91%; Mamata Bachupally to 89%; all other colleges are unaffected (their 0% loyalty means no scaling at all).

Minority & Women's colleges dropdown

  • 4 colleges (Shadan, Ayaan, Deccan, Dr. VRK Women's) only admit minority/women students via Management Quota — never real competitors for our 23 target colleges.
  • 2025 actual: 114 students joined those 4 colleges.
  • Shadan got a big real seat increase (150→250) for 2026 — modeled individually, not as one flat rate for all 4.
  • → Estimated 2026 capacity: 194 students (up from 114).
  • Turning "With" on removes a data-based share of these students from each college's pool, based on real overlap with that college's own historical range.
  • A college with no historical overlap (like Apollo) sees zero change.

MQ registration increase dropdown (stress test)

  • Not based on observed data — a dial you control yourself.
  • Tests: "what if more students register for MQ this year than in 2025?"
  • More applicants for the same seats → cutoffs can only go up or stay flat, never down.
  • "Same as 2025" = no adjustment.

Clearing a cutoff isn't a guarantee — the "~N within 5pts" note

  • A closing mark is just the weakest student who actually got in in 2025 — not a promise for anyone scoring the same or better.
  • Whether you actually get the seat also depends on: how many others at or above your score also want that specific college that round, and tie-breaking among students who share your exact score.
  • Using each college's real 2025 admission pattern, we estimated how many students typically clustered within 5 points of the final cutoff.
  • A low number (e.g. Medicity, ~5) means a fairly clean margin — clearing the mark is fairly decisive.
  • A high number (e.g. Kamineni LB Nagar, ~23) means many students bunch right at that score — clearing the historical mark is closer to a toss-up than a guarantee.

Putting it together — the feasibility rating

  • Very Safe — clears even Phase 1 (toughest).
  • Safe — clears Phase 2, maybe not Phase 1.
  • Borderline — only the final Phase 3 round would take you.
  • Very Risky — even Phase 3's mark is currently out of reach.
